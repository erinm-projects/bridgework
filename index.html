The bridge between employers and young talent is crumbling.

As opportunities for young people shrink, employers are questioning whether the next generation is ready for work. Young people, meanwhile, are questioning whether employers are willing to give them a chance.

The Project

We know there's a problem. Now we need to understand the relationship behind it.

Bridgework explores the tension between what the evidence shows, what employers believe and what young people actually experience, to understand where the relationship is breaking down and what it will take to mend it.


The numbers tell us what’s happening. Not why.


Behind the numbers is a relationship under strain. We need to understand what’s driving the disconnect between employers and young people, and where perceptions, experiences and reality are pulling them further apart.

When did hiring young talent start to feel like a risk?

The 2026 Young People and Work interim report , led by Alan Milburn, highlights a labour market that’s leaving young people behind. Entry-level routes are disappearing, while employers raise concerns around work-readiness, confidence and the support younger workers may need. Even apprenticeships are drifting away from the young people who need them most. But opportunity isn't disappearing equally. Where a young person starts, the networks they can access and the barriers they face can fundamentally change their route into work.

Research themes

The Lost Generation needs more than another report.

Bridgework brings employers and young people into the same conversation to explore what’s really happening between both sides: how perceptions translate into decisions, how those decisions are experienced, where expectations and reality don’t align, and what’s driving the growing divide. Because behind every headline about ‘young people’ are different starting points, different levels of access to opportunity and different experiences shaping their routes into and through work.

THE RESEARCH WILL EVOLVE

These themes give us a place to start, not a box to stay within. As the research progresses, we'll follow the stories, tensions and questions that emerge, allowing the project to explore new themes where the evidence, employer perspectives and young people's lived experiences take us.

01

Too risky to hire?

Why are some employers hesitant to take a chance on young talent?

And how does that hesitation affect the opportunities available to young people? We’ll explore the perceptions, experiences and business pressures shaping employer decisions, how different starting points and experiences might influence those perceptions, and what the evidence tells us about the perceived risks.

02

Ready for work, but whose version?

What does "work-ready" really mean? We'll explore what employers expect

from young people entering the workplace, what young people believe they need to succeed, and where differences in skills, confidence, expectations and support could be widening the divide.

03

Getting in. Staying in. Moving forward.

What happens when a young person does get the opportunity? We'll explore

the relationship beyond recruitment, from onboarding and support to belonging, performance and progression, looking at what young people need to thrive and what employers need to make early talent work.

04

Rebuilding confidence

What would it take for employers to feel confident taking a chance on young talent?

And for young people to feel there is a genuine place for them in the workforce? We'll explore what both sides need from each other and where practical changes could help rebuild trust, create opportunity and strengthen the future talent pipeline.

How the research works

Mixed-methods study

Bridgework combines qualitative and quantitative research to build a richer picture of the relationship between young people and employers, running from September 2026 to May 2027.

Qualitative discovery

The project begins with interviews and focus groups with young people and employers, before using quantitative research to test emerging themes at a wider scale.

Co-created research

Research is designed and delivered alongside the Bridgework Collective, a young project group who will be trained and supported by experienced researchers and practitioners to play an active role throughout the project.

Ethical by design

All research activity follows established ethical standards, including informed consent, anonymity, safeguarding and responsible data handling throughout.
